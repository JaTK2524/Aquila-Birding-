Noisy Miner

Quick Facts

  • Scientific name: Manorina melanocephala
  • Family: Meliphagidae
  • Size: About 28 cm
  • Weight: About 60-70 g
  • Wingspan: About 35-40 cm
  • Diet: Nectar, fruit, insects and other small invertebrates
  • Habitat: Open woodland, open forest, parks, gardens and suburban areas
  • Behaviour: Extremely social and territorial; colonies aggressively mob predators and competing birds
  • Activity: Diurnal
  • Nesting: A small cup-shaped nest built from grass, bark and spider web in a tree or shrub
  • Breeding Season: Usually August to January, with breeding influenced by local conditions
  • Clutch Size: Usually 2-4 eggs
  • Call: Loud, varied and repeated chattering calls
  • Lifespan: Usually several years in the wild
  • Conservation Status: Least Concern
  • Where to Find: Australia: mainly eastern and southeastern Australia, including Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria and South Australia

About the Bird

The Noisy Miner is a bold, highly social Australian honeyeater with a mostly grey body, black crown and cheeks, and a bright yellow bill and legs. Its name is extremely appropriate because colonies can produce a constant stream of loud calls. Noisy Miners are unusually aggressive toward other birds and may collectively mob much larger species such as hawks and kookaburras. They prefer open woodland and forests with relatively open understorey and have become common in parks, gardens and suburban areas. Despite the similar name, the Noisy Miner is completely unrelated to the introduced Common Myna, which belongs to the starling family.
