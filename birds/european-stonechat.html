European Stonechat

Quick Facts

  • Scientific name: Saxicola rubicola
  • Family: Muscicapidae
  • Size: About 11.5-13 cm
  • Weight: About 15.8 g
  • Wingspan: About 18-21 cm
  • Diet: Insects, spiders, larvae and other small invertebrates
  • Habitat: Heathland, grassland, coastal scrub, moorland and open areas with scattered bushes
  • Behaviour: Perches prominently and makes short flights to capture prey; often seen flicking its wings and tail
  • Activity: Diurnal
  • Nesting: A small cup nest hidden low in vegetation, often close to the ground
  • Breeding Season: Mainly March to June, with later broods possible
  • Clutch Size: 5-6 eggs
  • Call: Short, sharp calls; the song is a rapid series of clicking and musical notes
  • Lifespan: Usually a few years after reaching breeding age
  • Conservation Status: Least Concern
  • Where to Find: Europe: widespread across western, central and southern Europe; also North Africa and western Asia

About the Bird

The European Stonechat is a small, upright songbird usually seen perched prominently on bushes, fences or other exposed points. The male has a black head, white neck patch, orange-red breast and dark back, while the female is browner and less strongly marked. It is especially associated with heathland, rough grassland, coastal areas and scrub. Stonechats are active hunters that watch from a perch before dropping down to catch insects and other small prey. Many populations are resident or only partly migratory, while some birds move south in winter. It can be confused with the Whinchat, but the European Stonechat has a darker head and lacks the Whinchat's strong pale eyebrow.
