Pied Oystercatcher

Quick Facts

  • Scientific name: Haematopus longirostris
  • Family: Haematopodidae
  • Size: About 50 cm
  • Weight: About 500-800 g
  • Wingspan: About 80-90 cm
  • Diet: Molluscs, worms, crustaceans and other intertidal invertebrates
  • Habitat: Sandy beaches, mudflats, estuaries and coastal islands
  • Behaviour: Uses its powerful bill to prise open or hammer shellfish
  • Activity: Diurnal, with some feeding at night depending on tides
  • Nesting: A simple scrape on sand, shell or gravel above the high-tide line
  • Breeding Season: Winter to spring
  • Clutch Size: Usually 2-3 eggs
  • Call: Loud piping and whistling calls
  • Lifespan: Can live for more than 20 years
  • Conservation Status: Least Concern
  • Where to Find: Australia: Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria, Tasmania, South Australia, Western Australia and Northern Territory

About the Bird

The Pied Oystercatcher is a large Australian shorebird with black upperparts, white underparts, a long orange-red bill and pinkish-red legs. It forages along sandy and muddy shores, using its powerful bill to open shellfish and probe for other invertebrates.
